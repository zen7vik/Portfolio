## The problem

A customer wants to see its vendor's live risk posture. Both are tenants, but in different AWS accounts and regions, and the data is sensitive: risk scores, control assessments, findings. There was no path for this. I got the greenfield brief: build the service that moves this data safely, with consent, auditability, and without a human in the loop.

## The system

The service is Go, one binary with role flags (API, operations consumer, delay-retry consumer) so prod scales each role independently while local dev runs everything.

<Scrolly case="data-exchange" />

- **Consent model.** A contract records the relationship: who shares what with whom, with status lifecycle and bindings describing the shared slices. Creation is an idempotent conditional put.
- **Sync pipeline.** Three event types drive a run: generate (concurrently fetch risk, controls, and findings from upstream services, strip PII, upload versioned JSON artifacts), sync (copy files cross-account), ingest (create external findings in the consuming tenant). Retriable failures route through a delayed-message exchange with exponential backoff and jitter; poison messages land in a DLQ.
- **Cross-account security.** The copy step assumes a role in the source account via STS with a deterministic External ID derived from account and region, closing the confused-deputy hole: a leaked role ARN alone is useless.
- **Single-table DynamoDB.** Contracts and sync history share one table with composite keys. The secondary-index key attributes are written but the index is deliberately not provisioned; at current cardinality the rare reverse-direction lookup is a filtered scan, and promoting the attributes to a real GSI later is a zero-migration change.

## Key decisions

- **Cost-shaped schema.** Paying for a GSI to speed up a rare query path is backwards; denormalize the keys now, provision the index when cardinality demands it.
- **Explicit retry topology.** At-least-once with a delay-retry exchange and DLQ beats broker auto-requeue: you can see, cap, and reason about every retry.
- **Overlap prevention as data.** An active-sync marker on the contract makes concurrent runs impossible by construction, and only success advances the last-synced pointer.

## Outcomes

Shipped from empty repo to production across regions, owning architecture, eventing, deployment, and CI/CD. Ninety days in: zero error-budget breaches, and enterprise deals that required vendor-posture sharing unblocked.

## What I'd do differently

Provision the GSI up front; the scan trade-off was right on cost but wrong on operational surprise. And replace at-least-once ingest with a transactional outbox for true exactly-once semantics on the consuming side.
