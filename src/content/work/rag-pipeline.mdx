## The problem

Third-party risk runs on documents: SOC 2 reports, policies, filled questionnaires. Analysts were reading hundreds of pages to answer the same questions over and over. We wanted the platform to answer questionnaires from a vendor's own uploaded evidence, which means the unglamorous part had to exist first: a pipeline that safely ingests arbitrary customer files and turns them into something retrievable.

## The system

The service owns two domains: threaded comments on any platform entity, and the full evidence lifecycle.

<Scrolly case="rag-pipeline" />

- **Ingestion.** Clients upload directly to S3 via signed URLs, then confirm completion. A malware scan runs asynchronously; document status is the single source of truth across every async step, and a consumer-vs-API race on record creation is settled by document-UUID idempotency.
- **Vectorization.** Clean documents are chunked and embedded by an async consumer into AWS OpenSearch Serverless, indexed for hybrid retrieval: vector similarity plus keyword match, because compliance language is exactly where pure semantic search gets vague.
- **Answering.** A sibling service retrieves the relevant chunks from this index and drafts questionnaire answers through the internal multi-provider LLM gateway (GPT-4o/4.1, Claude on Bedrock), so model choice is a config concern, not an architecture change. The same index powers evidence summarization and classification.
- **Guardrails and tools.** I also shipped input guardrails for the workflow platform's AI nodes through the same gateway, and an MCP tool that exposes workflow analytics to the company's AI assistant.

## Key decisions

- **Status over orchestration.** Four async stages coordinate through explicit document status rather than a saga framework: simple, debuggable, and each consumer retries independently with backoff.
- **Hybrid retrieval from day one.** Vector-only search demoed well and failed on precise compliance phrasing; keyword+vector was measurably better on real questionnaires.
- **Gateway, not vendor lock.** Routing every LLM call through one internal gateway made provider swaps and guardrails a platform property.

## Outcomes

In production the pipeline processes about 25K malware scans, 10K completed uploads, and 7.6K embedding jobs a month, with an error rate of 0.03%. Analysts review drafted answers instead of writing them from scratch.

## What I'd do differently

Unify the dual-database support (MySQL and Postgres) that history left behind; the dialect abstraction invites subtle portability bugs. And I would add retrieval quality metrics earlier: you cannot tune what you do not measure.
